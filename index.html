How it works

Every play here is a real MLB play where the third base coach sent a runner home from second on a single to the outfield. You see the play develop, then the clock starts. Send him or hold him.

  • Send, and he scores: streak continues.
  • Hold, and he was thrown out in real life: streak continues.
  • Send, and he's out, or hold, and he scored: streak's over.
  • The clock runs out: streak's over. Coaches don't get a timeout.

Real coaches mostly send when it's safe, so the game draws safe and out plays about equally. Otherwise you could just send every time.

The model

After each call you'll see a second grade. A timing model estimates how long the runner needs to get home (from his Statcast sprint speed) and how long the ball takes to get there (from where it was fielded and the outfielder's arm strength). The gap between the two becomes a probability he scores.

Whether that probability is good enough depends on outs. With two outs an out at the plate barely costs anything, so you should send on a coin flip. With nobody out, holding gives you first and third with no outs, so you need to be close to sure. The thresholds come from a run expectancy table built from the same seasons, assuming the trailing runners take an extra base on the throw.

The streak is graded on what really happened. The model grades the decision. Sometimes the right call gets thrown out.

The animation

The field animation is built from the play data, not tracked from video, so think of it as a recreation. It always ends the way the real play did. The broadcast clip plays after you decide.
