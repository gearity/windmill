How it works

Every play here is a real MLB play where the third base coach sent a runner home from second on a single to the outfield. You watch the play develop, then the clock starts. Send him or hold him.

Your streak grows when you send a runner who scored, or hold one who was thrown out at the plate in real life. It ends when you send a runner who was thrown out, hold one who scored, or let the clock run out. Coaches don't get a timeout.

Real coaches mostly send when it's safe, so the game deals safe and out plays about equally. Otherwise you could send every time and never lose.

The model

After each call you get a second grade. A timing model estimates how long the runner needs to get home, from his Statcast sprint speed, and how long the ball needs to get there, from where it was fielded and the outfielder's arm. The gap between the two becomes the chance he scores.

How big a chance you need depends on outs. With two outs, an out at the plate costs little, so sending pays if he scores a bit under half the time. With nobody out, a hold leaves first and third with nobody out, so he needs to score about three times in four. Those thresholds come from a run expectancy table built from the same seasons, assuming the trailing runners each take an extra base on the throw.

The streak is graded on what really happened, and the model grades the decision. Sometimes the right call gets thrown out.

The animation

The field animation is a recreation built from the play data. It isn't tracked from video, and it always ends the way the real play did. The broadcast clip plays after you decide.
